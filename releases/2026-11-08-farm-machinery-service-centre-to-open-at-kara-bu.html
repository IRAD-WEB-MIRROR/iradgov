Farm-Machinery Service Centre to Open at Kara-Bulak Before Spring Sowing

Alatau Agro Machinery LLC has announced a US$3 million service and parts centre beside the Talas–Nurzhan road at Kara-Bulak, financed by Talasbank and the Rural Development Fund. The Ministry of Agriculture and Water Resources welcomed the project, which is due to open before the 2027 spring sowing.

Alatau Agro Machinery LLC, the Talas dealer that has sold and serviced John Deere tractors and combines since 1995, on Saturday announced a service and parts centre beside the Talas–Nurzhan road at Kara-Bulak, close to the Kara-Bulak Grain Logistics Depot.

The project is valued at US$3 million (IS 25.2 million at the official rate). About US$2 million is provided as a Talasbank loan, and the Rural Development Fund is co-financing about US$1 million. Alatau Agro Machinery is contributing the land and its existing workshop.

The centre is to have repair bays, a parts warehouse and a training room for mechanics, and will provide work for about 35 staff. The company has set the opening for the period before the 2027 spring sowing, with construction to proceed through the winter. Recruitment of mechanics and trainees will begin once building starts.

The announcement follows a record cotton harvest of 41,000 tonnes on the Fergana rim and the Chu, and a maize and potato harvest that has filled cellars and silos across the valleys. Farms in the Chu and Talas valleys have asked where their combines and tractors can be repaired before the next season begins.

The Minister of Agriculture and Water Resources, Melis Nurmatov, welcomed the project. "A good harvest is only as good as the machine that brings it in," the Minister said. "Farmers should know that a repair is a short drive away."

The Rural Development Fund, directed by Kanybek Zhorobekov, will take part in the project through its co-financing share. The Ministry of Agriculture and Water Resources will continue to follow preparations for the coming sowing season.
