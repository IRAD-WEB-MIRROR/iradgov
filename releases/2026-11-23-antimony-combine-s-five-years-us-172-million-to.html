Antimony Combine's Five Years: US$172 Million to the Budget in Three, and the Sary-Bulak Restart Under Way

The Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine's 2021–2025 management accounts show about US$905 million of revenue and US$172 million paid to the ISEZ budget in 2023–2025. The Combine's earnings are now funding the Sary-Bulak Restart Programme, which aims at about 420 permanent posts.

The Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine (IARMC), which operates the Sary-Bulak Antimony Works and the Chatkal smelter, has published a five-year summary of its management accounts for 2021–2025 on iarmc.com. The summary shows about US$905 million of revenue and about US$190 million of net profit, roughly 1.6 billion som at the Bank of Irad's official rate of 8.40 per dollar. As with the Combine's earlier yearly sets, the figures are management data and are not independently audited.

Revenue rose to US$140 million in 2023, US$236 million in 2024 and US$341 million in 2025, as prices strengthened after China tightened its antimony export controls. The two most recent years account for nearly two-thirds of the five-year total.

The Combine's payments to the budget of the Irad Special Economic Zone were US$34 million in 2023, US$56 million in 2024 and US$82 million in 2025, US$172 million in all. They were made as royalties, export duty and profit tax through the Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority, and amount to about a quarter of the Combine's revenue for those three years.

The earnings are now being put back into the works. Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat announced the Restart Programme to the labour collective at Sary-Bulak on 12 October. Its first stage costs 168 million som and aims at about 420 permanent posts. Recruitment opened on 26 October, with priority for residents of Talas and Sary-Bulak districts; company buses run from Talas. The Combine's two equipment and supply partners are to be named at signing before the end of the year. The Ministry of Natural Resources, headed by Minister Chingiz Asanov, is represented on the Combine's Supervisory Board.

The next public step is a hearing on the mercury recovery unit, which would need a new permit before it could resume work. It is due at the Rudnik house of culture on 26 November, and written comments are accepted until 10 December. The Mejlis budget committee is meanwhile preparing the 2027 draft budget.
