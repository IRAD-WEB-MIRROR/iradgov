Iradenergo to Study Floating Solar Pilot on Kyzyl-Adyr Reservoir

Iradenergo, the state power utility, will carry out a feasibility study of a pilot floating solar array of about 2 MW on the Kyzyl-Adyr Reservoir in Kara-Buura District. A donor fund is financing the study, which is put at about US$350,000.

Iradenergo, the state power utility, announced on Tuesday 24 November that it will study a small floating solar array on the Kyzyl-Adyr Reservoir in Kara-Buura District. A donor fund will finance the feasibility study, which is put at about US$350,000.

The working concept is a pilot of around 2 MW on the water. Iradenergo would purchase the output, as it already does from the Talas Solar Park on the Boz-Tektir terrace. The reservoir lies close to an existing grid line, which would keep connection work modest.

Iradenergo spokesman Vladislav Ten stressed the nature of the undertaking. "This is a study, not a decision to build," he said. Floating panels generate power around midday, while the winter evening peak between 18:00 and 22:00 continues to be carried by the Toktogul Hydroelectric Station and the Naryn cascade.

Toktogul remains the backbone of the zone's winter supply. The Toktogul reservoir stood at 81.5 per cent full on 24 November and is following its usual drawdown through the cold season. Because of the size of its seasonal swings in level, floating panels are being considered only for the smaller Kyzyl-Adyr water.

Kara-Buura District head Jyrgalbek Osmonaliev welcomed the study, noting that it would give the district a chance to contribute to the zone's power supply alongside the large hydro stations.

The study will also examine how an array would perform through the reservoir's yearly changes in level. The Ministry of Agriculture and Water Resources and the Talas basin irrigators will be consulted on the use of the reservoir water. Iradenergo will make the results public once the study is complete.
