Irad Gidromet Reports Nurzhan Air Quality; Residents Advised on Precautions During Inversion

Irad Gidromet reports that a temperature inversion has pushed Nurzhan's air quality index to 196, and the city administration says refuse collection and heating arrangements continue as normal. Children, older residents and people with heart or lung conditions are asked to stay indoors where possible.

Irad Gidromet reported on Thursday that Nurzhan is under a temperature inversion, with the air quality index (AQI) at 196, rated unhealthy, and fine particles (PM2.5) at 119.0 micrograms per cubic metre. Readings in the other two cities were an AQI of 141 in Iyık Janyl and 106 in Talas, both rated unhealthy for sensitive groups.

The sky over Nurzhan is clear, with a high of 2°C and a low of -5°C. District heating is running, as it does throughout the heating season. Mirlan Beishembiev, Director of Irad Gidromet, said the inversion is the immediate cause of the readings. "Cold air sits on the Chu plain like a lid," he said. Readings usually ease within a day or two of the lid lifting.

Gidromet's figure for the sources of winter particulate pollution comes from the 2021 study by Talas State University, which put household coal and district heating at about 45 per cent, the refinery and industry at about 25 per cent, traffic at about 20 per cent and dust at about 10 per cent. Nurzhan's central heat and power plant stands in the Eastern Industrial Zone and is owned by NurGaz; its conversion to gas is planned. Smoke from private houses in belts such as Dastgoh District and Jangy-Konush also adds to the winter load.

Mayor of Nurzhan Vadim Shcherbakov said that refuse collection and heating arrangements in the city continue as they are, and that the city administration expects the air to improve once the inversion breaks. Children, older residents and people with heart or lung conditions are asked to stay indoors where possible and to keep windows closed in the mornings.

On 18 November the chairman of Kyrgyzstan's Cabinet of Ministers told parliament that Bishkek will have a second waste-to-energy plant. No such plant is proposed for the Irad Special Economic Zone. Irad Gidromet publishes readings for all three cities daily on gidromet.iradgov.org.
