Camera Traps Record Two Snow Leopards in Upper Besh-Tash for the First Time in Decades

The Forestry and Protected Areas Service reports that camera traps in the upper Besh-Tash valley have recorded two snow leopards, the first confirmed there in decades. Rangers, Talas State University scientists and Akjele elders are discussing steps to keep the animals and the returning flocks apart.

Camera traps in the upper Besh-Tash valley have recorded two snow leopards moving along the high ridges, the first confirmed in that valley in decades, the Forestry and Protected Areas Service of the Ministry of Natural Resources announced on Friday.

The images are being read by the Besh-Tash Ranger Headquarters together with the Ilbirs Station, the research and rehabilitation station that Talas State University opened at Uuk-Bulak on 23 October. Dr Aizhan Torobekova, head of the station, said the pair most likely came from the Chatkal hills. For a snow leopard, she noted, the Chatkal Highlands and the Talas Alatau are one range, and the ridges above Besh-Tash are good ground for the species.

"A snow leopard returning to a valley is the best report that a protected area can receive," said Minister of Natural Resources Chingiz Asanov. The core zone of the Besh-Tash National Park covers about 11,000 hectares of its 32,400.

As the herds come down from the summer pastures, rangers are preparing measures to keep animals and flocks apart. They include night corrals for stock left on the high ground, a call line on which herders can report tracks or sightings, and regular checks of the camera traps. No livestock loss or incident has been reported.

The upper valley is tribal land, so the measures there depend on the agreement of the Akjele elders. Rangers and elders have been discussing the plan, and the talks are proceeding in a cooperative spirit.

The two animals are being observed by camera alone. No capture, collar or release is planned, and the Ilbirs Station asks walkers and herders not to go looking for them. The Ranger Headquarters will report again once the images have been fully analysed and the elders have given their answer on the herd-protection steps.
